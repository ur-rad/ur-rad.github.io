title: "Vantage-Point-Aware Planning"
abstract: "Robots increasingly operate in visible public spaces, where they must perform sensitive tasks under the watchful eyes of bystanders. To maintain security and privacy, these machines must actively manage human observation, which occasionally requires them to strategically deceive onlookers. However, existing robotic planning approaches fail to model human vantage points or represent intentional deception within their frameworks. This limitation creates an urgent need for vantage-point-aware languages, representations, and algorithms that allow robots to recognize they are being watched. This work introduces Vantage-Point-Aware Planning (VPAP) to determine whether current, off-the-shelf tools can handle these scenarios or if entirely new frameworks must be built. As a crucial first step, this work formally defines the problem to systematically uncover and target the remaining research gaps."
