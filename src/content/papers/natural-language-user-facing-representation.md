title: "Natural Language as a User-Facing Representation for Robotics"
abstract: "Robotic systems have traditionally relied on human-designed representations that make tasks and environments both explicit and computationally accessible. Recent advances suggest a different possible approach: natural language can itself serve as a shared, user-facing representation through which people specify and refine task-relevant knowledge. We argue that this role is particularly useful for concepts that are contextual, underspecified, or difficult to capture with fixed definitions or decision boundaries. We illustrate this idea through a disaster-response application in which a vision-language model must distinguish hazardous conditions from situations that are merely anomalous, a distinction whose operational meaning can be refined interactively through language. This process is a form of knowledge engineering: revising natural-language specifications changes how the system interprets the physical world. In this short paper, we suggest characterizing natural language as a representational layer in its own right, and describe language-based representations as a possible alternative to fully specified, human-crafted representations, particularly while task boundaries are being established. This perspective suggests that language can serve not only as an interface to robotic systems, but as part of the shared representational machinery through which both humans and agents construct and refine their understanding of the physical world."
